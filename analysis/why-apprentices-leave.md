# Why apprentices leave — two cases, and one mechanism nobody names

An open item from `frame-vs-evidence-log.md`, opened 2026-08-15. Nagual Anaam has himself said that analysing departures would be a good way to learn, so this follows his own suggestion.

Two departures were reconstructed in full from the apprentice-group archive: message-volume curves, the final months of interaction, the exit itself, and everything said about the person afterwards. They were chosen for contrast — one slow fade after four years, one abrupt exit after two — and because both have enough material to see a trajectory rather than a snapshot.

**The rule that licenses all of it was stated in 2021**, a year before the clearest case of it being applied. Verbatim, 2021-07-03:

> *"It's a great idea to really study why people leave apprenticeship. **It will show you how almost all the time the reasons are not what they seem.** But to the person who's had enough, they appear totally legitimate. The machine mind is the ultimate spin doctor 🔄 Beware of its skewed news reports of the outside world in your head. Learn soberness, strategy and hard pragmatic perception. Ask questions 👍🏽😺"*

Read as advice it is unremarkable — people do rationalise. Read as a standing rule it does something else: **it establishes in advance that any reason a leaver gives is not the real reason.** Which means the group can study departures indefinitely, as it is invited to, without a single stated reason ever counting as data. That is exactly what happened when `arlo` said the apprenticeship was not helping him heal and the answer came back as gratitude. The invitation to study departures and the rule that departures cannot be studied from what leavers say are the same message.

**Roster context.** 27 people posted in the group in 2021; 6 of them still posted in 2026. Thirty-one distinct people have posted essays over the archive's life; the current roster is about ten. Departure is the normal outcome, not the exception, which is what makes the group's explanations for it worth examining.

## Case 1 — orin: four years, then a fade

**Span:** Oct 2020 → Jan 2025 (4y3m), 1,090 messages. The longest-tenured departure in the archive.

**The curve.** Peak engagement Dec 2023 – May 2024 (70, 53, 71, 41, 37, 40 messages/month), then a six-month decay: 17, 4, 6, 4, 3, 1, 1. A fade, not a break.

**What the last year looks like from inside.** Her role in the group was almost entirely asking other people questions. Three things recur:

- Her questions repeatedly met irritation. One apprentice: *"I was just being self-important, self-obsessed and irritated by the fact that you were asking me questions, while having your own agenda… I felt like my time is being wasted"* (2024-07-23), followed two days later by an apology. Another exchange ends *"I see, thanks for your efforts. Be well and have a great day"* (2024-09-29).
- The teacher closed one of her lines of questioning directly — *"This one is now closed. No need to pursue further"* (2024-06-05) — and later asked how often she read others' essays (2024-08-07). Her answer: about five in a month, deliberately, as *"a part of my self-prescribed withdrawing of all kinds of information… This is not dis-interest, it is temporary mind-cleansing. This group is where I feel most connected to others as I lose more and more connection to the average world. Please let me know if this is ok. 🙏😳"*
- Health and life-stage were visibly in play: a body she described as needing a "senior" program before she could return to jiu jitsu, a naturopath, a paused certification, and an active search for somewhere new to live.

**One month before leaving** she posted her most grateful message in the archive: the apprenticeship got her off cigarettes — *"30,000 cigarettes… HALF a LITER of tar… I can't thank you enough… 🙏🙏🙏 for having changed me"* (2024-12-01). **She left while crediting it with a major, verifiable life change.** Whatever her reasons were, "it didn't work" is not among them.

**The exit** (2025-01-03), in full: *"Dear all, I thank you for the joined toltec journey, for the feelings and insights, the vulnerable and the strong moments we shared. For me it's time to go. I wish you a powerful growth. I'll see you in the masterclasses, be well, blessed and impeccable."* No reason given. Four apprentices said goodbye. She stayed a masterclass attendee.

**The two post-mortems.** Eight days later an apprentice asked the teacher directly: *"what can be learned from orin's leaving?"* The complete answer:

> *"Never take on anyone older than 40yrs as apprentice. 👍🏽😊 Too much repetition of the machine mind has gone in the older someone is. Having said that, I'll probably be taking on someone who's over 50 soon 🤣"*

An attribute of her category, self-contradicted in the same message. An older apprentice immediately thanked him for taking on her *"old ass"*, and the group moved on within the half hour.

The second post-mortem is doctrinal, and more important. holt's essay that week reports a standing rule: *"It reminded me of what Nagual Anaam once said when [another apprentice] left: **that apprentices that do not focus on inner silence will eventually leave sooner or later.**"* He then applies it to her: *"although it is difficult to judge from my limited perspective because she rarely interacted… it didn't seem that her inner silence had grown much over the years."*

**This is a pre-installed, unfalsifiable explanation for any departure**: the cause is a deficiency in an unobservable quality, asserted while admitting it can't be judged, and requiring no information about the person's actual reasons — which the author states outright he doesn't have.

**What happened when two people noticed.** Two apprentices independently pressed exactly that point: *"I am curious to know if you had any reason for the need to mention orin's growth in inner silence even though you state that 'it's difficult to judge'?"* The teacher's response was not to answer but to hand holt a script, in the group: *"he's pulling the conversation into a mental debate - ask him: If you focused on the idea behind my words would you still not get what I was trying to convey?"* — and for the second questioner, *"she's doing the same so ask her the same but word it slightly differently obv to avoid machine like behaviour and imitate nature instead."* holt posted both, near-verbatim. Both questioners conceded. The teacher's summary: *"It saves precious time attention and energy if we just get people to focus on what's actually important… The body goes where the head goes. Control the head and you control the body."*

There is a legitimate version of "look past the words to the idea." Here it retired a valid logical objection to a claim about why someone left, by script, with the coaching concealed on purpose.

## Case 2 — tove: the penalty spiral

**Span:** Oct 2023 → Nov 2025 (2y1m), 628 messages. **No fade at all** — 28 messages in her second-to-last month, 17 in her last, then removal. A cliff.

**The mechanism, dated.** This is a financial-ultimatum sequence over unwritten essays, running eleven weeks:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2025-09-01 | Missed two essays without communicating. Penalty raised **"to £200 per missed essay for you specifically my dear."** Told she is *"self sabotaging"* and in a *"self destructive loop"*, and that *"other black apprentices [sena] & AlmaSi suffered exactly the same fate"* |
| 2025-09-02 | Reminded she had promised to leave if it recurred; asked *"By when will you have paid £400 penalty?"* She announces her departure. Called *"a cheap joke"* and *"an absolute twat"* in the group. Her partner is told her leaving would damage **his** strategy |
| 2025-09-03 | She retracts, thanks him, asks to stay |
| 2025-09-27 → 09-30 | Interrogated on daily screen-time, then on how long since she last proactively wrote to anyone. *"Tbh - I'm almost through with you… you're on your last legs here my dear"* |
| 2025-10 → 11 | Her most engaged period: 28 messages in October, apologises to her partner in the group, takes a market stall, sells 80% of her stock, posts essays, gets warm praise from four apprentices |
| 2025-11-18 | Missed another essay. Penalty **£350** for that one, **£650 owed** in total. *"I have no idea why someone would literally flush their hard earned money down the toilet… Goes even beyond stupidity"*, and to the group: **"What a stupid female! 🤷🏽‍♂️"** She asks for one more chance; gets *"tomorrow's call to convince me"* |
| 2025-11-19 | *"I will remove you end of day today from groups. May you have a fruitful break period. Hopefully see you again 👍🏽"* Her reply: *"thank you for being. You have left me so much more better than you have found me."* |

**Total cash penalties in her final eleven weeks: £1,050** (£400 + £650), on top of £150 per call, while working a job she disliked in Namibia and raising a child. Another apprentice was penalised £150 while planning to sleep in her car for the winter, so this is not unique to her — see the money hypothesis below.

**The framing at exit.** Two things stand out. First, *"be aware that the apprenticeship does not make anyone grow. It's what the apprentice does with the apprenticeship that makes him/her grow… She will be fine with or without the apprenticeship just as anyone here who leaves"* — a claim that insulates the apprenticeship from the outcome in both directions at once. Second, her partner (`dell`, still an apprentice) was asked *"what's your part in this?"* and answered entirely in terms of his own strategy: a gap would open between them, tension, possibly separation, costing him time and energy, and their daughter would suffer. That was assessed as a *"good assessment."*

**Afterwards.** It was called a "break"; she never came back. Six days later another apprentice submitting a late essay wrote: *"I understand how this mirrors [tove]'s situation and I really don't wish for the same fate. I apologise… I've sent the fee 🙏🏻"* — **her name had become a disciplinary example within a week.** By mid-2026 she had moved to Germany to finish nursing studies, and her leaving is discussed in the group mainly as material for her partner's growth — *"you'll find ways to make it beneficial and grow through [tove] leaving for a year."*

## Case 3 — `hale`: left without permission, and what the group did next

**Span:** Dec 2020 → Apr 2022 (16 months), 871 messages. Chosen because his exit is the only one in the archive that breaks the group's procedure, which is how the procedure becomes visible.

**The disciplinary run-up.**
- **2022-02-15** — a post of his is dismissed as *"Irrelevant because no one cares"*, and the group is asked *"How can `hale` present it in a way that isn't passive aggressive juvenile petulance?"*
- **2022-03-11** — *"`hale` could you remind me of any agreement we have made regarding you not having had any sessions for many many months now?"* He had stopped taking paid calls.
- **2022-03-18** — he and `dov` are put on *"escalated impeccability. Miss one more self reflection for the next 2 months and I will simply remove you from the apprenticeship no questions no warning."*
- **2022-04-04** — the pile-on below.
- **2022-04-18** — he leaves without a word.

**What he actually did, in full.** Another apprentice had written about receiving financial help. He replied to her, warmly:

> *"The ranting is awesome, I do it for the entertainment value and the laughs I get from the people that are listening to my rants. **I am pleased to hear that your financial burden felt some relief via the help you got.** Thanks for sharing! 🙏🤘"*

The phrase *"financial burden"* was identified as *"adverse magic"* — burden rather than challenge — and the response was scripted for the group:

> *"Check the adverse magic here! It isn't a burden - it's a challenge. @[apprentice] - copy/paste this to him: **Fuck off `hale` - I completely reject your adverse magic description born out of the average man's ignorance and good intentions. Money is NOT a burden for me. Don't repeat this again in your dealings with me.** 😺👍🏽"*

One minute later: *"In fact, **EVERYONE** copy paste the above and tag `hale` into it 😺 This will help everyone. **`hale` you too - type this here to yourself.**"*

**Five apprentices posted it verbatim within four hours** — one softened it to *"Hey `hale`, fuck off, please"* — and the group then carried straight on praising each other's essays in the next messages. He was still expected to type it at himself.

He left fourteen days later. The group's explanations afterwards were defiance, wanting to get rises out of people, and an interest in *"left handed magic"*. **Nobody mentioned the ultimatum, the unpaid sessions, or the pile-on that five of them had taken part in two weeks earlier** — which is the most complete example in the archive of a group producing an account of a departure that omits everything the group did.

For the avoidance of a cheap reading: the underlying idea being taught — that describing money as a burden reinforces a relationship to money you don't want — is a real one, and several apprentices credit that kind of language discipline with genuine changes. That is not what is at issue. What is at issue is that the teaching method was a scripted five-person "fuck off", and that fourteen days later the resulting exit was explained entirely by his character.

**The reaction, in order.** The teacher's first message is not about why he left:

> *"Is there another active group where everyone hangs out? 🤔 `hale`'s left and no one seems to be too bothered 🤔 I remember `sena` had started a group independent of here after running it by me. **How many ex apprentices are part of that group?** Nothing wrong with that but it's good for me to be remain informed."*

He asks twice more in the next ten minutes — *"How many ex-apprentices are still hanging around this other group?"* — and delegates the count to `sena`. Two apprentices go and check the membership list of the other group and report back. `sena` describes the standing practice: *"Usually after someone goes through their farewells here, I give a few days for them to exit on their own and if they don't, then I remove them from that group too."*

Then the norm is stated outright: *"I usually give anyone who leaves 24 hours to say their farewells here. `hale` chose to leave immediately without any communication which is what has made me curious."* And once it is established that he is gone from the side group too: *"Ah! That makes sense - that's why that disrespectful fool was able to leave here with impunity. The irony 😸😸"*

**What this shows.** Departures are managed events with a 24-hour window, and adjacent spaces are cleared of ex-apprentices as a matter of routine — so **ex-members do not accumulate anywhere the current members can reach them.** That is the structural reason nobody in this group has ever heard a leaver's account of leaving. It is also why the archive's picture of departures is entirely composed of what was said about people after they were gone.

**The eulogies.** Within hours, six apprentices posted assessments. Two are ordinary human warmth. The pattern in the rest is the group norm applied to a person: *"I am not sorry to see `hale` go… **So I will use this as an opportunity to reflect on my own self importance**"*; *"I would like to make his leaving a useful thing"*; and one apprentice reporting she had predicted it because he *"had more interest in left handed magic studies than an interest in becoming a better human."* `arlo` alone gave a substantive account — that he liked him, would have bonded with him in person, and was concerned about his defiance of the law. **Nobody asked whether the ultimatum a month earlier had anything to do with it.**

## Case 4 — `arlo`: the group's one financial success story, who left twice

**Span:** Dec 2020 → Dec 2022, with a four-month exit in between: 1,145 messages, 55,800 words — the most substantive contributor among the leavers. Chosen because he is the single most important case in the archive for the money question.

**The disciplinary arc.**
- **Dec 2021** — named in the group as already on the way out for unsubmitted essays: *"Brett & `arlo` are already going out. I don't want time wasters here."*
- **Dec 2021, days later** — the hunt. **What triggered it:** in a casual late-night exchange about acting "like a lion", he observed that acting like one has consequences nowadays, then added *"I'm just venting… in most respects, I'm still just a little lamb"*, said he could not stand the current US administration, that he would never vote for that party again, and that he unexpectedly missed the previous president. That is the whole offence — a vent about domestic politics, self-labelled as venting.

 The response, in sequence: *"Then either: 1. Move to another country like `taro` if you have half the balls 2. Seek to turn it into an advantage **3. Kill yourself and be done with it.** I made video about people like you `arlo`…"* — the "move to another country" line being a comparison to an apprentice who had emigrated, offered as the courageous alternative to complaining about your government. Then:

 > *"Your inability to move your assemblage point away from the whining victim is exactly the reason why **you are on a break for six months**. All other apprentices - **here's a scared little rabbit to hunt and kill. Go**"*
 > *"Time to be brutally honest with this guy"*
 > *"The newer ones - comment on what black magic you saw `arlo` cast on himself with his politics post"*

 Apprentices complied. `holt` — today the group's most senior member — posted five consecutive messages, including *"unless you tell us evidence of your lionness, that thought is worthless"*, *"is this again just a thought you are masturbating in your head"*, and *"noone cares if you miss [the president] or if you can't stand the new govt administration… what makes you believe you are so important"*, signing off *"Bless you `arlo`, all the best to you!"* The hunt is closed with *"Excellent hunt everyone! Well done `arlo`"* and the note that *"No change has actually occurred. NONE!"*

 **So a six-month break was imposed as a sanction for a political vent.** Correction to the first version of this file: the break does not appear to have been enforced as a removal — he kept posting throughout, so the essay gap from May to September 2022 is his first departure, not the sanction. What the "break" amounted to in practice is unclear from text.
- **Jan 2022** — tasked with compiling a list of "the fictional real" that *"should easily run it into the millions minimum"*, due in seven weeks.
- **May 2022 — the first exit, and this is the important part.** He left properly, with a farewell: *"I've decided to quit the apprenticeship. Thank you Nagual Anaam for all your support and ideas… The best to you all on your journey's!"* Two apprentices asked him why. He declined the first — *"I'd rather not go into that"* — and then answered the second, in the group, in writing:

 > *"I have not given up on becoming a warrior. It's just that over the past few months **my health conditions have become worse**, and every-time I have a flare up of pain, it sets me back so much and it's very demotivating. I've had so many setbacks because of this. **I just don't feel this apprenticeship is helping me to heal at this time.**"*

 A week later an apprentice asked *"is there something that can be learned from `arlo`'s leaving?"* The answer: *"`arlo` thanked me as an apprentice for making him rich, for pointing him towards tai chi and for his renewed interest in music. **What would you thank me for if you were to leave?** 😊"*, and then the lesson: *"Those who have this to thank the nagual for, they gain - those who are focused on everything but this will leave 👍🏽😊 **This is why `arlo` left.**"*

 **The stated reason was in the group chat, eight days old, and unambiguous — the apprenticeship was not helping him heal. The official explanation became insufficient gratitude.** This is not a case of a group lacking information about why someone left. The information was there and was replaced. See the entry in `frame-vs-evidence-log.md`.
- **Sept 2022** — he comes back, and is celebrated: *"`arlo`'s broken a few records around here: **First apprentice to go from 0 to hero in terms of money.** First apprentice to come back – usually if they gown they gown 😸"*
- **Oct–Nov 2022, within ten weeks of returning** — the tasks resume. A hunt prompt aimed at him (*"Someone ask `arlo` - what part of a hunt does the deer like? … can the deer escape the hunt by: 1. Telling the lion that the lion does not know what it it like to be in the deer's shoes"*), an instruction to re-read a book twice by year end, an instruction to *"find the earliest and nearest VIPASSANA retreat… and go do it ASAP"*, and an instruction to complete **EFT practitioner training up to level 3** with certification dates to be acknowledged within a day. All of it landing on a man who had just left over unmanageable chronic pain, and all of it costing money and time.
- **Dec 2022** — he leaves again, silently, without using the 24-hour window this time. His last messages are ordinary and warm: a question about chai, his Christmas plans with family, praise for another apprentice.
- **Jan 2023** — `orin` asks *"so `arlo` is gone? May I ask what happened?"* The complete answer: *"People come and go my lovely - 🤷🏽‍♂️ **Not everyone is mentally & spiritually charged in the correct way to pursue power.** If the struggling victim runs the show, better to walk away and forget about it."*

**Two exits, two incompatible explanations, both about him.** The first was insufficient gratitude; the second was being wrongly charged and a "struggling victim". Neither mentions the essay ultimatum, the hunt, or the impossible task. And the man described in January 2023 as a struggling victim is the same man described in September 2022 as the group's first apprentice to go from zero to hero with money.

**Why this case decides part of the SSE question.** He is the only apprentice the teacher has ever explicitly credited with getting rich — and the money came from **trading the stock market**, not from self-employment. In his own words, two months after being celebrated for it:

> *"I've not always thought about the money I've made in the stock market as SSE… I'm not actually running a business, I'm not offering my services to others. I'm just sitting in my room listening to financial and economic intellects… but is it, really? It certainly doesn't give me a sense of long-term stability nor does it give me the same sense of satisfaction as I'm sure `taro` and others feel when making something with their hands."*

**And "0 to hero" is not supported by anything he said about his own finances.** No figure appears anywhere in the archive, and his own descriptions run the other way: a portfolio that swung with growth-stock cycles, worry about money that he tracked as correlating with his chronic pain, *"If I had a shit ton of money in the market, then I could adjust my portfolio to mostly lower risk stuff, **but I'm not at that point yet**"* (Jan 2022), a health-insurance plan he had cancelled because $600 a month was too much, a 13-year-old car and an 8-year-old laptop kept because they still work. He did leave a teaching job in 2020 to trade full time, which is real and is more than anyone else in the archive achieved — but there is **no evidence of a millionaire**, and the phrase was the teacher's, not his.

So the flagship financial success is (1) not self-employment, (2) doubted as such by the person who achieved it, (3) unsupported by any figure, (4) achieved by someone who had been publicly hunted with "kill yourself" as option three and sanctioned with a six-month break for a political vent, and (5) recategorised as a spiritual failure four months after being celebrated. That is the whole SSE question in one apprentice, and it is followed up in `sse-outcomes.md`.

## Case 5 — `sena`: praised, spared, and gone the same week

**Span:** Mar 2021 → Dec 2022 (21 months), 842 messages, **69,854 words — the most prolific writer in the archive.** She ran the group's independent side chat and was, by the teacher's own description, an established apprentice.

**Standing before the exit.** In Jan 2022 she was congratulated in the group as *"the very first certified Kukuwa instructor in her city"* — a real, dated, external credential, and one of the few genuine path-with-heart achievements anyone reached. In Apr 2022 she was the person asked to audit how many ex-apprentices were sitting in her side group, and she described her own practice of removing them once their farewells were done. She was, in other words, trusted with the group's boundary maintenance.

**The exit, in three days.**
- **2022-12-12** — *"`sena` when was the last time you put a self reflection in"*
- **2022-12-13** — with `dov`: *"By end of day today. Tomorrow if no reply - you're on a self assessment break for 3 months. If you haven't got your head straight about this by then, you're out. Not running a juvenile detention centre here - should've replied to this first time like emotionally mature adults."*
- **2022-12-14** — her reply: *"My apologies… I have been far from impeccable… **It has been 89 days since the submission of my last reflection.** I thank you very, very much for this hammer tap to check me back to sobriety… I ask to please have 72 hours to get back on track… I am far from an emotionally mature ad[ult]"*
- Response: *"Perfect reply 😺👍🏽 **Well done for avoiding the cleaver** `sena` 😁"*

**And that is her last message in six years of archive.** She complied, was praised for complying, was explicitly spared — and never wrote again. The 72 hours she asked for passed in silence; **she was removed by the admin on 2023-01-14**, a month later. Nobody appears to have followed up in between, and no one asked about her afterwards.

**Why this case matters to the money question.** She is one of the three Black women the teacher later grouped as having *"suffered exactly the same fate"* — but she lived in a major US city and there is **no sign of fee arrears in her case at all.** Her trigger was essays, not money. That refines the earlier finding: of the three, one exit was purely financial, one was financial and essays together, and one was essays alone. The common factor across all three is the **ultimatum**, not the poverty — which is what the base-rate test on Monday is designed to settle.

## Case 6 — `gale`: the departure nobody mentioned

**Span:** Feb 2022 → Mar 2023 (13 months), 338 messages, 23 essays. Included as the null case, because what is absent here is the finding.

- **2023-02-15** — given a task: everyone in the group writes *"as if they are sending office emails"*, and he is to *"break this silly flow by communicating in the opposite way to everyone here from now on."*
- **2023-02-26** — *"are you aware that there's a £50 penalty for not submitting essays on time without prior communication? 😊"*
- **2023-02-27** — *"👆🏽👆🏽 Won't ask again"*
- **2023-03-03 to 03-05** — his last messages are warm and ordinary: detailed appreciative feedback on another apprentice's essay, a considered answer about dividing the world into authentic and fake, and a farewell to a newcomer: *"All Best to you Pratik :)"*
- Then nothing.

**No farewell, no removal announcement, no explanation, and — searching the entire archive from his last message to today — not one mention of his name by anyone, ever again.** Six years, thirty-one people, and a thirteen-month apprentice simply ceases to appear. The 24-hour farewell norm was not invoked. No apprentice asked what had happened to him, as they did for `orin` and `arlo`.

So the group's account of `gale`'s departure is that there is none. That is worth recording precisely because the other cases produce explanations so readily: **the explanations appear when someone asks, and whether anyone asks appears to depend on how embedded the person was**, not on what happened to them.

## The membership ledger — 40 removals, 2 self-departures

Telegram keeps a service-message record of every join and removal, independent of anything anyone says. Pulled in full to `tg-private/membership-ledger.txt`:

- **40 removals. 38 of them were performed by the admin account. Two were people leaving of their own accord.**
- 38 additions plus 11 joins by link, across 36 distinct users added and 37 removed.

**This changes how every departure in this file should be read.** The language throughout the archive is that people "leave", "give up", "walk away", are "not charged in the correct way" — and the record says that in 38 out of 40 cases somebody else pressed the button. The 24-hour farewell norm is not a description of people departing; it is a courtesy window offered before a removal. `hale`'s offence of *"choosing to leave immediately without any communication"* was, in the ledger's terms, one of the only two occasions anyone actually left.

**Dates for the cases in this file**, all verified against the ledger rather than inferred:

Two corrections to earlier sections of this file follow from it. **`sena` was not spared and then vanished in the same week** — she was spared on 14 December, said nothing for a month, and was removed on 14 January. And `arlo`'s second exit was a **removal on 2 January 2023, the same day** `orin` posted *"so `arlo` is gone? May I ask what happened?"* — she was reacting to the service message. The shrug she received was in response to a removal the teacher had just performed.

**Re-admissions are also in the ledger, and they are more common than the group's language suggests.** `arlo` re-added 2022-09-02 after four months out. `fenn` **removed 2024-02-12 and re-added 2024-06-06** — a four-month removal that reads in the chat as a voluntary absence. `sable` **removed 2025-07-19, re-added 2025-09-16.** `isla` similarly. So "break" sometimes means a removal with a return, and the two are not distinguishable from the conversation alone.

**And the churn at the bottom is invisible in the chat.** People added and removed within weeks: 13 days (Sept 2023), 10 days (June–July 2026), six weeks (Nov–Dec 2024), two months (Jan–Mar 2025), three months (2024), three and a half months (`naia`, 2025). None of these produced a post-mortem, and most produced no message at all.

**Nobody renamed themselves out of the record.** Checked by Telegram user id rather than display name: `sena`, `gale`, `hale`, `arlo`, `orin`, `tove` and `mira` have not posted in the group under any name since the dates above.

## The cross-case finding

**The proximate cause of departure in this archive is legible, dated, and never named by the group: a financial-penalty ultimatum over unsubmitted essays.** Traceable cases, all with the same shape — ultimatum or penalty, then an apologetic promise to reform, then exit within 1–10 weeks:

- `sena`, Dec 2022: 89 days without reflections → *"By end of day today. Tomorrow if no reply - you're on a self assessment break for 3 months. If you haven't got your head straight about this by then, you're out."* She apologised, asked for 72 hours, committed to reform, was praised for *"avoiding the cleaver"* — and **that is her last message in the archive.**
- `gale`, Feb 2023: asked about a £50 penalty — gone within a month.
- `bram`, Nov 2023: told to calculate his total penalty for missing essays "for such a long time" by end of day — gone within two weeks.
- `naia`, Aug 2025: removed three weeks after offering an extra £50 as a *"peace offering"* over a one-day-late essay.
- `tove`, Nov 2025 — above.
- `hale`, Mar 2022: *"escalated impeccability… miss one more self reflection and I will simply remove you, no questions no warning"* — gone in one month, without notice.
- `dov`, Jan 2023: *"post the details of your giving task of power for November and December by end of day tomorrow. If you don't - then I will remove you from here and you will be on a self assessment break till end of March"* — he had already stopped posting three weeks earlier and never posted again.
- `arlo`, Dec 2021: named in the group as *"already going out"* over essays, hunted days later — left five months later, returned, left again.

Against that, the explanations the group produces are **category attributes and unobservables**: age over 40; insufficient inner silence; *"choosing drama over sobriety"* and female entitlement/"Gollum Power"; *"other black apprentices suffered exactly the same fate"*; *"victim of one's own strategy"*; insufficient gratitude; *"not… charged in the correct way to pursue power"*; a *"disrespectful fool"*; more interested in *"left handed magic"* than in becoming better. **Nine departures, nine explanations, all of them properties of the person who left.** Not one prompted a question about whether anything in the group's own arrangements contributed — and in the two cases where an apprentice did ask directly (*"what can be learned from `orin`'s leaving?"*, *"so `arlo` is gone? May I ask what happened?"*) the answers were the age rule and a shrug.

The nearest thing to a structural question anyone asked was about the *reasoning* in a post-mortem, and it was answered with a script the teacher supplied.

**And the leavers cannot be reached.** Farewells get a 24-hour window; ex-apprentices are then cleared out of adjacent groups as routine hygiene, a practice stated plainly in `hale`'s case. So no current member has access to any leaver's own account of why they left, which is precisely why the archive's picture of departure consists entirely of what was said about people after they had gone. Anyone inside the group who wanted to check the group's explanations would have no way to do it.

Note also what the essay-penalty rule selects for: it makes *written output* the condition of membership, so the people it removes are disproportionately those with the least spare time and money — and the archive of "what the apprenticeship achieves" is then written by whoever survived that filter. The survivorship problem isn't incidental to the essays; the essays are the filter.

**Result: 5 of 13 (38%), or 6 counting `rex` at exactly 12 weeks.** `arlo` was added on

really frustrates me. I think to myself, is it possible that other apprentices are working
harder than me? The answer I get is No."* He did not leave inside 12 weeks; he left at ~21, and
then returned, and then vanished. **So the enforcement repertoire is wider than fines and
ultimatums — suspension is in it — and my sweep patterns do not cover it.** Expect the true
enforcement group to be larger than 13.

So the honest finding is the one I did not expect:

> **Penalty-or-ultimatum is not a mechanism that reliably produces departure.** Seven of the
> twelve did not leave inside 12 weeks, and **four are still in the group between 15 months
> and 20 months after their enforcement event.** `kesh` absorbed a £150 penalty and stayed
> another 18 months. `sable` was asked about a £50 penalty and is still here. Stated as a
> general law — penalised, therefore gone — the claim is false, and the earlier section
> overstated it by listing only the cases that fit.

**What survives, and it is sharper than what it replaces.** The five fast exits are not the
five biggest fines. They are the five cases where the enforcement took the form of **a public
demand for an immediate answer, under an explicit threat of removal, with a same-day or
next-day deadline**: *"by end of day today, tomorrow you're out"*; *"Answer @…"*; *"miss one
more and I will simply remove you"*; a direct question about a penalty put to someone in
front of everyone. The seven who stayed were, with the exception of `rex`, handed **a standing
charge they could pay or absorb** — £50, £150, a forfeiture clause, a remark about a chopping
block — with no immediate public reply demanded.

So the better-specified hypothesis, replacing the old one:

> It is not the money that precedes departure. It is **being required to answer publicly,
> immediately, with removal named as the alternative.** The fine is survivable; the summons
> is not.

That is testable on the next cohort and it makes a prediction: an apprentice given a standing
penalty will usually stay, and an apprentice given a same-day public ultimatum will usually
go, regardless of the amounts.

**Three limits, stated rather than buried.**

1. **The comparison group is contaminated, and this is the big one.** I can only see
   enforcement that happened *in the group chat*. Fees and penalties are largely handled on
   the paid calls, which are not in the archive. So "no documented enforcement" does not mean
   "never enforced", and any base rate computed from the other group understates enforcement
   there. The 42% is a figure about **publicly visible** enforcement only.
2. **Last message is a proxy for departure, and an imperfect one.** `rex` posted for the last
   time in Nov 2021 but was still being addressed as a member in Mar 2022 — so he was silent
   inside the group for months before leaving it. Where the membership ledger gives an exact
   removal date it should be preferred; for the rest, these weeks are lower bounds on tenure.
3. **N = 12.** Nothing here supports a percentage to more than one significant figure, and a
   single additional case moves it by eight points.

**What this does not touch.** The rest of the section above stands: 38 of 40 exits were
admin removals, the explanations offered afterwards are properties of the person who left,
the leavers cannot be reached, and the essay-penalty rule makes written output the condition
of membership. Those findings are about what is *said and done* around departures. This test
was only about whether penalties *cause* them, and the answer is: not on their own.

## The race question, precisely

Yar raised it: `sena`, `AlmaSi` and `tove` were all Black women, and he senses a high correlation. What the archive supports, and what it doesn't:

**Evidenced.**
1. **The teacher asserted the pattern himself**, as an explanation: *"Other black apprentices [sena] & AlmaSi suffered exactly the same fate."* A category-level causal claim about three named people, offered as instruction.
2. **A racialised register exists in the group.** Same teacher, Dec 2022, on employing people from outside the EU: *"especially from a fuck all place like Africa."* `tove` was also, in her final weeks, working through a self-description about *"a typical representation of an average black woman"* — language she uses about herself after two years in the group, and which nobody questioned.
3. **The penalty escalation applied to `tove` is the harshest in the archive** — £200 then £350 per essay, *"for you specifically"*, against £50 as the standing rate for others.
4. **Two of the three cases share an identical exit mechanism**, verified independently of the teacher's claim.

**Not evidenced.**
1. **N = 3, and one of the three isn't in this archive at all.** No message by or about "AlmaSi" exists in the apprentice-group export under that spelling; her case is currently only the teacher's assertion.
2. **The mechanism is not race-specific.** The same ultimatum-and-penalty sequence was applied to European and American apprentices (£50 penalties, "chopping block" warnings, a €1,000 forfeiture threat, one removal), and at least three of them left within weeks too.
3. So the correlation as stated — being a Black woman caused the exit — isn't established. What *is* established is that the **explanation** offered for those exits was racialised, which is a different and better-documented claim.

**The better-specified hypothesis: currency.** Penalties and call fees are denominated in GBP and applied at flat rates to people with radically unequal incomes — a corporate finance professional in Luxembourg, a woodcarver in Japan, a software engineer in Spain, a soldier, a comedian, and an apprentice on Namibian wages raising a child. £650 is not the same object in Windhoek as in Luxembourg. This predicts exactly the observed pattern without needing race as a cause, it explains the European exits too, and it is testable: rank every traceable departure by apparent capacity to absorb GBP penalties and see how well that orders the exits.

### Who is actually in the room

`yar`'s observation that this is a first-world club, with him the only member from a poorer country, is broadly supported by the archive — country by residence, with confidence varying: UK (teacher, fees in GBP), Denmark, France, Switzerland, Luxembourg/Latvia, Germany→Japan, a NATO member state's army, the US (several), the UK again, and — the exceptions — Kenya and Namibia. The filter therefore has two stages, and the second is the invisible one: **£150 a call plus GBP penalties means most of the world cannot enter at all**, so the surviving evidence about what the apprenticeship does comes from people for whom those sums are affordable. Worth measuring properly: fees as a share of median local income per apprentice's country.

## What to check next

- **`sena`'s case properly.** She left Dec 2022 with 842 messages and 70k words in the archive — enough for a full reconstruction. (AlmaSi is now identified as `mira`; see the update above.)
- **Fees as a share of median local income**, per apprentice's country. This is the sharpest available test of the money hypothesis and needs no new data from the group.
- **Why enforcement lands where it does.** Now that the submission record is flat across the group, the discretion is the variable. **Lead candidate, agreed with `yar`: debt owed to the teacher.** Both money-driven removals were of people carrying debt for fees or calls — `mira` selling motorbikes, `tove` working a call-centre job for six months specifically to repay call debt, and then being penalised for lacking the time that job consumed. That is a foreseeable outcome of the arrangement, not a surprise about her character. The test: for every apprentice, whether they were ever in arrears, against whether they were ever penalised or given an ultimatum. Other predictors to check alongside it: whether the person pushes back, partner status inside the group, tenure, and country.
- **What leavers are told privately.** All of the above is group-visible material. Calls are where the actual exit conversation happens, and none are in the archive.
- **Whether the penalty rule predicts departure better than anything else.** This is a clean, falsifiable test on the existing corpus: for every apprentice who was ever penalised or given an ultimatum, how many left within 12 weeks — against the base rate for those never penalised. If the ultimatum is the best predictor, then "why apprentices leave" has a mechanical answer that no post-mortem in the group has ever mentioned.
