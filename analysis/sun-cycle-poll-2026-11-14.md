A small watch note, not a finding. It records who answered what, so the answers can be read
again later against what each person actually achieves.

## The poll

- **Source:** TAC, message #36751, 2026-11-14 11:35 UTC. Votes are public (non-anonymous poll).
- **Question:** *"How many hours of life do successful people learn to manage in one sun cycle?"*
- **Options:** 8, 12, 16, 24, 26 and above.
- **Context:** posted two minutes after an apprentice (`wynn`) explained she has not painted for
  a year because her teaching, freelance and study commitments fill her days. The teacher had just
  replied *"There will always be other things to do in life"* and assigned a daily painting timer.
  So the poll reads as a reply to "I have no time".
- **The teacher's own vote:** 26 and above. No explanation given. Answers are not expected to be revealed.

## Readings of "26 and above"

The question gives no way to tell these apart:

1. **Leverage:** delegation, employees, systems. Hours other people work count toward yours.
2. **Rhetoric:** "you have more time than you think", meaning a push against excuses.
3. **Frame:** a successful person goes past ordinary limits, so the answer is above the physical maximum.

It does **not** score the abundance prediction (`prediction-2026-11-06-abundance.md`), because
nothing here names apprentice income or effort. That changes only if an explanation comes later.

## What to watch

The literal answer to the question is at most 24 hours. The useful question is whether the
answer someone gives tracks the results they get. Check later, using the same outcome measures
as `sse-outcomes.md`: income from their own work, a business or project that ships, and
commitments kept (`commitments-vs-outcomes.md`).

- At the time of the poll, externally checkable evidence of success is thin for most of the
  voters, including the 26+ group. Record it as a baseline, not a verdict.
- **Revisit:** 2027-05-14. For each voter, write down any dated and verifiable result since the
  poll, and note whether the 26+ voters show more of it than the 8–16 voters.
- If the teacher ever explains the answer, quote it here and check it against reading 1.
