## 1. Who he was

- **Name:** Frederick Joseph Eikerenkoetter II. Born 1 June 1935, Ridgeland, South Carolina.
  Died 28 July 2009 in Los Angeles, aged 74.
- **Death:** he had a stroke in 2007 and never fully recovered. A stroke is a brain event, not a
  heart attack. The cause and type of his stroke were never published.
- **Family:** married Eula M. Dent; one son, Xavier, who runs the legacy ministry (revikelegacy.com).
- **Teaching: "Science of Living."** Pentecostal style mixed with 19th-century New Thought and
  Spiritualist metaphysics. God is in you; your mind is your God-power; the Bible is a manual for
  health, happiness and money. He also ran a "Business of Living Institute" ("Thinkonomics").
- **Slogans:** "The *lack* of money is the root of all evil." "You can't lose with the stuff I
  use." "Close your eyes and see green… money up to your armpits" (NYT, 1972). "Change makes your
  minister nervous in the service", meaning send bills, not coins.
- **Books:**
  - *Rev. Ike's Secrets for Health, Joy and Prosperity — For YOU!*, his own.
  - *Reverend Ike: An Extraordinary Life of Influence*, by Xavier Eikerenkoetter and Mark Victor
    Hansen.
- **YouTube:** @RevIkeLegacy (sermons, the "Joy of Living" TV show; claims 500+ lessons) and
  "Rev. Ike Legacy Media" (interviews). Good first watch: *Will the Real Jesus Please Stand Up?*

## 2. The early years, 1950–1969

| Years | What he did | What he was selling |
|---|---|---|
| 1950 (age 14) | Assistant pastor, Bible Way Church (Pentecostal), Ridgeland; traveling preaching and faith healing on the Sea Islands | healing |
| 1956 | Bachelor of theology, American Bible College, Chicago | — |
| 1956–58 | Air Force chaplain's assistant (an enlisted support job) | — |
| Aug 1962 | Founded United Church of Jesus Christ for All People, Beaufort, South Carolina | charismatic church |
| 1964 | Boston, "Miracle Temple" | faith healing: "it was the big thing at the time"; "snatching people out of wheelchairs and off their crutches, pouring some oil over them" |
| 1966 | New York, rented the Sunset theater, 125th St, Harlem | **switched to prosperity**, aimed at "a more striving, stable, middle-class audience" |
| late 1960s | Hundreds of radio stations; cash donations; prayer cloths mailed out | prosperity |
| 1969 | Bought the Loew's 175th Street Theatre and renamed it the Palace Cathedral (now the United Palace) | — |

Takeaways:
- **The message followed the market:** healing while healing sold, prosperity once he went after
  a richer audience.
- **The brand came from a constraint:** the Harlem theater's sign was too narrow for
  "Eikerenkoetter", so it became "Rev. Ike". I found no logo design.
- **Scale came from radio and mail,** about 3 years (1966–69). Healing kept him going for
  about 15 years before that. I have no income figures for the healing years.
- **Radio:** I couldn't find how he got his first slots. Radio preachers of that era usually
  bought airtime and paid for it with listener donations. *That is an inference, not verified.*

**The building price:**
- Sources disagree: $500k (Historynet), "over half a million" (Wikipedia), $600k (Grokipedia).
- That is about **$4.5–5.5M** in 2026 consumer prices.
- Grokipedia (a weaker source) says half was a $300k mortgage from the seller, Loew's, so cash
  needed was about $300k (≈ $2.5M today).
- The money came from congregation pledges and offerings, mostly from poor and working-class
  Black parishioners, plus his mail and radio income.

## 3. Business model

1. **Broadcasting as the funnel:** about 1,700–1,770 radio and TV stations at the 1970s peak.
   The sermons were free.
2. **The "Blessing Plan":** listeners sent money and got blessings back, promised that their own
   prosperity would increase.
3. **Direct mail:** prayer cloths sent to donors. Charm letters (yarn, a piece of "prayer rug")
   told people to mail the item back **by the next day** with **$20–30** so he could bless it,
   warning that not doing so **could bring doom**. Run from the United Christian Evangelistic
   Association office in Brookline, Boston, the "financial engine". An unaudited "educated
   estimate": **at least $500k a month** (Boston Globe, 1999). *Whether the blessed item went
   back to the sender: not found.*
4. **Courses and lectures:** Science of Living, "The Power of Fascination", mental
   reconditioning, Thinkonomics; plus books and booklets.
5. **The palace** as the flagship that made it all look real.
6. **His own wealth as the product demo.** He told the congregation openly that they paid for his
   Rolls-Royce and his mansion.

**Legal structure:** churches, not companies.
- US churches are automatically tax-exempt, and the IRS can audit them only under special
  restrictions (IRC §7611).
- The Brookline property ($1.8M) was fully tax-exempt and every donation was tax-free (Boston
  Globe, 1999).
- His Science of Living Institute and Seminary awarded degrees to himself, his wife and his son.

**Lifestyle:**
- Joked about owning "10 or 12" Rolls-Royces, elsewhere 16 ("My garage runneth over"), reportedly
  with mink interiors.
- Flashy suits, jewelry, processed hair.
- Mansions on the East and West coasts, world cruises, a velvet throne in the church.
- His estate at death was "several million dollars".

## 4. Criticism

- The core complaint: he preyed on the poor and spent their donations on cars, clothes and homes.
- The targets were mainly the elderly, the poor, and people of African and Caribbean descent.
- The fear-based mail ("doom" if you don't send it back) is what critics singled out.
- The **IRS and US Postal Service** investigated him. In 1999 the Suffolk County District Attorney
  opened a fraud investigation.
- **No charges or convictions found;** sources say he survived every investigation.

## 6. The "successful success" question

Rev. Ike got rich by *selling* the method (donations, the Blessing Plan, mail), not by applying it
to some other business. *The Secret* and *Think and Grow Rich* follow the same shape: the method
reliably pays the one who sells it.

For Nagual, per `yar`: his income is mainly private calls, plus masterclasses (recently made
donation-based). Structurally that's the same pattern, the teaching pays the teacher, but on a
small scale and without fear-based fundraising. By `yar`'s account his own "path with heart" has
not made much money.

The fairer test is the apprentices: see `analysis/sse-outcomes.md`, including the 2026-11-06
update (`vela` dropped her job; `fenn` partly back at the brewery).

### Verdict (2026-11-06, `yar` agrees)

"Successful success" = (1) the method pays the preacher and (2) does not pay the followers.

1. **Pays the preacher: yes in structure, no in scale ("not yet").** The reliable money flows
   from students to the teacher, as with Rev. Ike. But it's a modest living, with no jackpot.
2. **Doesn't pay the followers: mostly yes so far, on the money promise.**
   - Zero confirmed salary-replacing businesses in about six years.
   - `vela` is the first candidate (cannot count before about 2027-08).
   - `fenn` is partly back in employment.
   - `yar` is prospering through IT employment, without doing the gift task.
3. **Against the label:**
   - No sign it's run deliberately as a funnel: no fear-based fundraising, no wealth display,
     and masterclasses are now donation-based.
   - Real non-money gains: apprentices moving from a job to work they like.

**One line:** on money, the structure of "successful success" without the success. Not the
Rev. Ike con, but the prosperity method has not yet been shown to work for anyone who follows
it, the teacher included.

**What would change the verdict:**
- `vela` still covering her living costs from her own practice in late 2027.
- Nagual earning meaningful money from something other than teaching.
- The two new apprentices (recently joined, per `yar`) are the next cohort to watch.
