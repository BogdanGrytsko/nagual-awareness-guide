# Rev. Ike: 5-minute talk prep (Yar)

Started 2026-11-12. The format is a 5-minute timer, Yar speaks, about 650–700 words. Facts are in
`rev-ike.md`. This file holds only the talk.

## Lines to keep

- "He had no product. He sold a transaction: give to me, and it comes back to you multiplied.
  That's hope with a mechanism attached, plus proof, which was his own Rolls-Royce."
- "He sold the promise that giving to him would make you rich. And the only person that promise
  was ever proven for… was him."
- Opener for the origin: "He started at 14 as a faith healer, pouring oil on people in
  wheelchairs, and only switched to money in his thirties, when money sold better."

## Shape (draft)

1. Hook: one phrase, then a pause.
2. Faith healer at 14, then the switch to money (one sentence).
3. The teaching, set against Yar's own life (see below).
4. The business model: the transaction; the mail-outs.
5. Death: "God in me is my health" vs. a stroke at about 72 and death at 74.
6. Conclusion: what he keeps and what he drops. Memorize the last line and stop. No "so yeah,
   that's it."

## Yar's phrases (from the videos, 2026-11-13)

- I feel God stretching out in me.
- If success and prosperity aren't added onto you then something is wrong with your religion!
- The person who believes in himself is a god in action.
- Whatever you add to I Am is added onto you.
- To pray means to invoke a feeling of that good of what you desire.
- Your faith is no good until you learn to have faith in yourself.
- God is the presence and power within me right now.
- I'm worthy of all God's goodness.

Wants to cover the spiritual side too, not only money and health.

## Facts and how to phrase them

- Mail-outs: say "The Boston Globe reported" (Brian McGrory, 1999-01-22). The yarn or prayer-rug
  piece went back with $20–30; not returning it "could bring doom".
- $500k a month: unattributed "educated estimates". Say "estimated at", or drop it.
- DA = District Attorney (Suffolk County, Boston, Ralph C. Martin II) opened a fraud
  investigation in 1999. **No charges found.** Say "investigated", never "charged" or
  "convicted".
- Quotes from aggregators (weak sources; check in a sermon if one becomes central): "pie in the
  sky… now with a cherry on top"; "the best thing you can do for the poor is not to be one of
  them"; "not important what other people believe about you".

## Yar's answers to the teaching (2026-11-12)

- Now vs. later: he mostly accumulates. Delayed gratification bought the cash house in Spain and
  maybe the exit from Ukraine. Open question: where is the line? Gran Canaria depends on the
  friends' visit on Nov 30; he'll go anyway.
- Spending: feels nothing. Learned from the stock rollercoaster (essay) that a 5–20k paper loss
  only means more patience.
- Display: keeps quiet. Unchallenged belief: "they'll charge me extra". Inner reaction: "big cars,
  small dicks". Wants to reflect more on this.
- Visualization: a black car with his wife and a female friend. That is display.
- Gift task: never found a recipient who met the criteria.
- Money was never his main problem.
- Display, follow-up: he buys mid-to-high quality, but no "who-knows-what" premiums. Exceptions:
  Lacoste T-shirts ($65). He drives a black VW Tiguan, "not THAT black, not THAT jeep". He suspects
  he doesn't fully allow it. "Charged extra" is not the real reason (he never deals with
  tradespeople). His wife knows his income only roughly.
- Gran Canaria: decide at the Nov 30 visit; if the friends say no, book on Dec 1 anyway.
- Untangling the fantasy: the car alone is flat; the two women with no car are "live and
  kicking"; the car on display is "whatever". So the charge is desire, not display. On the
  big-car guy: "he's compensating, he feels desperately lonely". "Desperately lonely" produced a
  reaction in his throat (not quite a sting).
- **Father (the core of the talk, disclosed 2026-11-12):** he never spent money except on a new
  Baptist church in Ukraine (US-imported) and dressed like a homeless man. The family lived
  "fucking poor". After he died, his job passed to Yar's mom (two jobs) and they became far
  wealthier. Yar's conclusion: a big part of his father's salary went to that church, and he got
  special treatment there. His father's former wife saw the preacher in a bar with a young woman
  on his lap; his father called her a liar. Yar: "I fucking hate those two-faced preachers."
- Father, continued: died of cancer at 48, with his health declining long before. Official story:
  nobody knew until a few months before. His first wife saw him barely walking at their son's
  birthday party and got him to hospital. Yar's mom hadn't noticed (she had a 5-month-old, Yar's
  brother). Yar's guess at what he got from the church: hope. Yar has asked his mom what she
  thinks (answer pending).
- His father never saw a doctor. His motto was never to go to doctors, and doctors were used as
  bogeymen to scare Yar as a child. Unknown whether the rule came from the church or from his
  father himself; don't attribute it to the church in the talk.
- Decision: the father goes in the talk, in the middle. End on positive notes from Rev. Ike.
- Pleasure purchases: Calvin Klein and other good clothes in Ukraine a few years ago felt nice.
- Home: part of the "charged extra" belief is about his wife ("she'd want me to spend double on
  her").
- Candidate talk line: "Rev. Ike had his pie now, with a cherry on top. His followers paid for it
  with their later."
