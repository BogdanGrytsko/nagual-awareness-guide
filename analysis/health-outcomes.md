# Health — who got ill, who got better, and who got blamed

The fourth standing question, opened 2026-08-15 at `yar`'s request. Designed, not yet run; rules frozen before scoring, as in the other files.

## The question

> Across thirty-one apprentices and six years: how many reported health problems, how many reported getting better, how many got worse — and **how was each outcome explained?**

Illness is a weaker instrument than money, because it is confounded in ways money is not (age, self-selection, base rates), but it is far stronger than inner silence, because it produces **dates, diagnoses, procedures and irreversible facts.** A hearing loss either resolved or it didn't.

**The fairness rule, stated first.** In any group of thirty-one adults over six years, serious illness is *expected*. Finding illness here proves nothing whatsoever about the apprenticeship, and any write-up implying otherwise is invalid. What is testable is narrower and more interesting:

1. **Attribution** — what the illness is said to be caused by.
2. **Care-seeking** — whether medical care was sought, delayed, or discouraged.
3. **Asymmetry** — whether recoveries are credited to the training while failures to recover are credited to the person.
4. **Outcome anchoring** — whether reported improvements have external anchors (a resolved condition, a test result, a quit addiction) or consist of reinterpreted symptoms.

## The mechanism is curriculum, not incidental

This is the central fact for the whole vector: **Louise Hay's *You Can Heal Your Life* is assigned reading, cover to cover three times, with chapter-by-chapter essays.** The instruction from Nov 2020 is explicit, including how to handle the book's *"dis-ease table"* — the appendix mapping specific physical conditions to specific mental causes. Every long-serving apprentice has processed that framework three times and written on it.

So when an apprentice attributes their illness to their own emotional pattern, they are not improvising. They are applying the syllabus. That has to be stated plainly before any case is read, because it means the attribution pattern is **the expected output of the teaching**, and the question is what it costs.

## Cases already visible

- **`fenn` — permanent hearing loss.** Oct 2023: he came down with flu and *"as I got better, my ear didn't. **According to Louise Hay I was stubborn and refusing to listen to my higher self / conscience.** Makes sense now, but at the time I was deep in denial and had a hard time figuring out what exactly I was refusing to listen to."* By June 2024: *"I may have suffered an injury that I cannot recover from… my hearing on one ear is not what it was before, nor is my balance."* He did get rehabilitative exercises from doctors, so care was sought. He also converted the residual tinnitus into a guidance system: *"there's a whooshing sound coming almost constantly from my ear… I know my body is telling me something, and I often take them as **confirmations of the thought I'm having**."* Planned treatment as of Dec 2024 was EFT tapping. **Note the confound honestly: he had smoked heavily for years and was still smoking through this period** — and quitting later is one of the group's genuine health wins.
- **`arlo` — chronic pain, and the reason he left.** Years of unexplained groin pain plus persistent abdominal pain, which he explicitly tracked as correlating with his money worries. He cancelled a $600/month health insurance plan, later took a $500/month plan with a $6,100 deductible. He tried EFT, Theta, acupuncture. He left in May 2022 saying *"my health conditions have become worse… I just don't feel this apprenticeship is helping me to heal at this time"* — see `frame-vs-evidence-log.md` for what the group did with that.
- **`rook` — kidney stones.** Jan 2025, after a medical check: *"it confirmed what I already knew. That **the kidney stones were only a reflection of my emotional imbalances**."* Care was sought; the causal account was emotional.
- **`orin` — a body she described as needing a "senior" programme** before she could return to jiu jitsu: walking, Falun Gong, tai chi, meridian tapping, Reiki, a naturopath, a paused certification because she needed a break. She left after four years. **And she is also the clearest positive case:** she quit smoking through the apprenticeship — *"30,000 cigarettes… HALF a LITER of tar"* — and credited it unreservedly a month before leaving.
- **`holt` — a strong positive claim**: *"I'd literally be on medication by now for sure without his guidance"* (Apr 2026). Worth taking seriously and worth checking against what he wrote at the time.

## What gets scored

Per apprentice: conditions reported with dates; whether medical care was sought, delayed or avoided; the stated cause each time; the outcome at last mention; and whether the outcome had an external anchor. Then the four axes above, tallied.

**Two things to count that will be easy to miss:**
- **Quitting addictions is a real, large health outcome**, and where one has actually happened it belongs in the tally at full weight — `orin`'s smoking cessation is a Tier A win by any medical standard, self-reported but specific and sustained over four years. **Check each claimed quit against later messages before recording it**: the first draft of this table logged a second cessation that never happened. `fenn` has been stating the intention to quit for at least two and a half years and was still smoking in the last month of the archive, which makes his case an entry for `commitments-vs-outcomes.md`, not a health win.
- **Symptom reinterpretation is not recovery.** Tinnitus experienced as guidance is the same tinnitus. This is the single easiest place for the archive to look like it contains improvements that it doesn't.

## Confounders, stated in advance

- **Self-selection.** People often arrive at a self-development apprenticeship *because* something is already wrong, including physically. Illness at intake is not an outcome.
- **Age.** The cohort spans roughly 25 to over 50; the older members carry higher base rates.
- **Reporting asymmetry.** The group rewards celebration, so improvements are reported enthusiastically and deteriorations get reported as learning material or not at all. Absence of reported worsening is weak evidence.
- **Smoking, drinking, weight and diet** are live in several cases and are ordinary causes of ordinary illness. Where they are present they get named, in both directions.
- **The teacher's medical stance is a variable, not a background.** It includes strong anti-vaccination positions, promotion of an unvaccinated-only social platform, disapproval of pill-seeking, a Q-Link pendant, avoidance of microwaves and Bluetooth, and dietary rules (*"bring down your grains consumption to no more than 10% of your overall eating if you value your life and well being"*). Whether that produced delayed care in any specific case is exactly what the archive can answer — and so far every case examined **did** involve doctors, which is evidence against the strongest version of the concern and should be reported as such.
