# Dreams: doubled dreams, interpreters, and how to become one

Reading list put together on 2026-11-15. It started from Pharaoh's doubled dream (Genesis 41:32:
*"for that the dream was doubled unto Pharaoh twice; it is because the thing is established by
God"*), then moved on to learning to interpret dreams yourself, rather than relying on a paid
interpreter.

All references were written from memory. The Bible and Berakhot references are reliable. The
ones marked *check* need verifying before you quote them.

## Doubled dreams in other sources (short list)

- **Genesis 37:5–11**: Joseph's own two dreams, sheaves then stars, with the same meaning. They came
  true about 22 years later.
- **Berakhot 55b**: a repeated dream is one of the dreams that "come true", with Genesis 41:32 as
  the proof text.
- **Herodotus 1.107–108**: Astyages has two dreams about his daughter Mandane, the flood and the
  vine. Both foretell Cyrus.
- **Herodotus 7.12–18**: Xerxes has the same dream twice, and Artabanus has it too when he sleeps
  in Xerxes' bed. Xerxes invades Greece and it ends in disaster. A counter-example: the doubled dream led to ruin.
- **Cicero, *De Divinatione* 1.57** (*check*): the two travellers at Megara. Chaucer retells it
  in the Nun's Priest's Tale.
- **Gilgamesh**: two dreams, the meteor and the axe, both meaning Enkidu is coming.
- **Hadith on the adhan**: Abdullah ibn Zayd and Umar had the same dream. **Laylat al-Qadr**:
  "I see your dreams have agreed."
- **Genesis 47:13–26**: what happened after Pharaoh's dream. People gave their money, then their
  livestock, then their land, then themselves. Pharaoh took a fifth of every harvest from then on.

## Bible

**Genesis: the Joseph cycle (start here)**
- 37:5–11: Joseph's two dreams
- 40: the cupbearer and the baker. 40:8: *"Do not interpretations belong to God? tell me them"*
- 41: Pharaoh's doubled dream. Key verses 41:16, 25, 32
- 42:6–9: the brothers bow, and "Joseph remembered the dreams"
- 47:13–26: the aftermath

**Other Genesis dreams**
- 20:3–7: Abimelech is warned in a dream
- 28:10–22: Jacob's ladder at Bethel
- 31:10–13, 24: Jacob's dream about the flocks; Laban is warned

**Rules about dreams**
- Numbers 12:6–8: God speaks to prophets in dreams, but to Moses directly
- Deuteronomy 13:1–5: the dreamer whose sign comes true is still rejected if the dream leads
  people astray
- Ecclesiastes 5:3, 5:7: *"a dream cometh through the multitude of business"*. This is the
  "leftover from the day" type of dream.
- Jeremiah 23:25–32: against false dreamers. 23:28: *"What is the chaff to the wheat?"*
- Jeremiah 29:8: don't trust dreams "which ye cause to be dreamed"

**Interpreters and how they worked**
- Judges 7:9–15: Gideon overhears a Midianite's dream, and an ordinary soldier interprets it
- 1 Kings 3:5–15: Solomon sacrifices at Gibeon, then dreams that night. Close to incubation.
  The second appearance is in 9:2.
- 1 Samuel 28:6, 15: God does not answer Saul "by dreams"; dreams are one channel of answers
- Job 4:12–17; 33:14–18: night visions, and "God speaketh once, yea twice"

**Daniel (the main text)**
- 1: the ten-day diet of vegetables and water. 1:17: "understanding in all visions and dreams"
- 2: he must tell the king's dream before interpreting it, and gets it in his own night vision
  (2:19)
- 4: Nebuchadnezzar's tree dream
- 5:12: "interpreting of dreams, and shewing of hard sentences"
- 7:1: *"he wrote the dream, and told the sum of the matters"*. Daniel writes his dream down.
- 10:2–3: three weeks without meat, wine or "pleasant bread" before a vision

**New Testament**
- Matthew 1:20–24; 2:12–22: Mary's husband Joseph acts on four dreams, each one immediately
- Matthew 27:19: Pilate's wife's dream
- Acts 16:9–10: Paul's vision of the man from Macedonia, acted on at once
- (Acts 9 and 10: Saul and Ananias, then Peter and Cornelius. Paired visions in two places that
  only make sense together.)

## Talmud: Berakhot 55a–57b (the dream chapter)

Free with English at sefaria.org (search "Berakhot 55a").

- **55a**: "A dream not interpreted is like a letter not read"; "no grain without straw"
  (quoting Jeremiah 23:28); for a good or bad dream, "the joy, or the sadness, is enough"
- **55b**:
  - Which dreams come true: a dream seen in the morning, a dream a friend had about you, a dream
    interpreted inside the dream, and ("some add") a dream that repeats (Genesis 41:32)
  - "All dreams follow the mouth": the interpretation shapes what happens
  - Rabbi Bana'ah took one dream to all 24 interpreters in Jerusalem, got 24 different readings,
    and all of them came true
  - "A person is shown only the thoughts of his heart". Nobody dreams of a golden palm tree or
    an elephant going through the eye of a needle.
  - A good dream can take up to 22 years to come true (Joseph)
  - Turning a bad dream to good: tell it to three friends, who answer "Good it is, and good may
    it be…"
  - The prayer said during the priestly blessing: "Master of the world, I am Yours and my dreams
    are Yours…"
- **56a**: **Bar Hedya**, the interpreter who gave good readings to clients who paid and bad
  readings to clients who didn't. Abaye and Rava dreamed the same dreams and both sets of
  readings came true. Also the **Caesar story**: Rabbi Yehoshua tells the emperor what he will
  dream, the emperor thinks about it all day, and he dreams it (a dream deliberately induced).
- **56b–57b**: the dream-symbol dictionary. It shows *how* meanings were built from verses and
  wordplay (a well means peace, after Isaac's wells in Genesis 26), which you can copy to build
  your own.
- **57b**: "A dream is one-sixtieth of prophecy"

**Elsewhere in the Talmud**
- Shabbat 11a: fasting after a bad dream ("as effective as fire on flax")
- Sanhedrin 30a (*check*): "Words of dreams neither raise nor lower", meaning dreams carry no
  legal weight
- Jerusalem Talmud, Ma'aser Sheni 4:6 (*check*): more stories of dream interpreters

## The dream-question practice (she'elat chalom) and dream journals

You don't wait for a dream; you put a question before sleep and ask for the answer in the dream.

- **Hai Gaon** (Babylonia, around 1000 CE) was asked how dream questions were done. In his reply
  he describes the practice without ruling it out: fasting, purification, reciting divine names.
- **Jacob of Marvège** (Provence, 13th century) put legal questions to dreams and recorded the
  answers in *She'elot u-Teshuvot min ha-Shamayim* ("Responsa from Heaven"). The standard edition
  is Reuven Margaliot's, in Hebrew. Some answers contradicted accepted law, so later authorities
  refused to rule by dreams: *"lo bashamayim hi"* ("it is not in heaven", Deuteronomy 30:12). Dream
  answers have to pass the same tests as any other answer.
- **Common elements** in the sources (the details vary): fast during the day, immerse in a ritual
  bath, wear clean or white clothes, sleep alone, recite psalms or divine names, and ask **one
  clearly worded question**. Some later versions write the question down before sleep.
- **Joshua Trachtenberg, *Jewish Magic and Superstition* (1939)**, the dreams chapter. In
  English, covers the dream question and Hai Gaon. Free at sacred-texts.com.
- **Hayyim Vital, *Sefer HaHezyonot*** (Book of Visions): a 16th-century kabbalist's dream diary
  from Safed, kept for years. English in Morris Faierstein, *Jewish Mystical Autobiographies*
  (1999).

## The method: data + outcomes

**Artemidorus of Daldis, *Oneirocritica*** (2nd century CE), the one surviving ancient manual for
the job, partly written to train his son.
- He collected dreams at markets and festivals and **followed up on what actually happened**.
  Book 5 lists 95 dreams with their outcomes.
- He separated *enhypnion* (a dream about your current state, which predicts nothing) from
  *oneiros* (a dream about the future). Islam has a similar three-way split: from God, from
  Satan, and *hadith al-nafs* ("the self talking to itself").
- Know the dreamer: the same dream means different things for a slave, a rich man or someone ill.
- Some dreams come true literally; others are symbolic and need decoding.
- Translations: **Daniel Harris-McCoy (Oxford, 2012)**, with the Greek alongside; Robert J. White
  (1975).

For comparison: the Book of Dream Interpretation (*Kitab al-Ta'bir*) in Sahih al-Bukhari.

## Putting it into practice

1. **Before sleep**: write down one specific question, with the date.
2. **On waking**: write down the dream before you do anything else.
3. **The same morning**: write your reading, before you know how things turn out.
4. **Later**: check what happened and score yourself. Keep dreams that look like leftovers from
   the day separate from the ones that look like signal.

After some months this is your own "Book 5", and it tells you whether you're a good interpreter.
The same discipline as the dated, sealed predictions elsewhere in this repo.
