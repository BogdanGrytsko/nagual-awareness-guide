# Successful self-employment — the outcome the teaching can be scored on

The third standing question, opened 2026-08-15 at `yar`'s request, and in one respect the most important: **successful self-employment (SSE) is the only major prescribed outcome that produces externally checkable evidence.** Inner silence is unobservable; personal power is unobservable; whether a gift "opens doors of abundance" is unfalsifiable. Money is none of those things. Someone either left the job or didn't, and either replaced the income or didn't.

Status: **RUN 2026-08-17.** Rules were frozen before scoring, as in `commitments-vs-outcomes.md`; results are in the RESULTS section below and the design above is left unedited.

## The question

> Apprentices are pushed to leave the "slave job", find the path with heart, and gather resources in a way they love. **After five years and thirty-one people, what did that produce?**

`sable` is reported to have raised the group-level version of this herself some time ago — that nobody in the apprenticeship has become "Bruce Lee", that no millionaires have come out of SSE. That observation has not yet been located in the chat archive; it may be in an essay, a call, or one of the sub-groups. Locating it is the first task, because **a member noticing a group-level outcome failure is itself a candidate falsifier event** — and what happened next is the thing to check. `yar`'s recollection stands as testimony until then.

## What has to be pinned down first: the promise

The study is worthless without the claim. Before scoring anyone, extract from the archive what was actually promised about SSE and on what timescale — the teacher's own statements, dated. Without that, any result is re-readable as "it takes as long as it takes", and the question dies as unfalsifiable. If no timescale was ever stated, **that is itself the finding**, and it should be reported in those words.

`yar`'s recollection is that the timescales were **vague, something in the region of five to ten years**, and he notes it is currently unverifiable. That is the honest starting position, and it has a consequence worth stating in advance: on a ten-year horizon almost nobody in the group is yet due, so the only apprentices whose outcomes can be scored at all are the ones who have been in long enough — `holt` (5+ years), `vela`, `isla`, `kesh` (5 years, now gone), `fenn`, and the leavers from the 2020–22 cohort. **A vague long horizon also means the claim cannot fail during the period in which most people leave** — which is itself a structural feature worth recording rather than an accident.

## Outcome ladder

Scored on money, because that is what can be checked:

- **L0** — still employed, no SSE income
- **L1** — SSE income exists but is far below subsistence (market stalls, occasional gigs, one-off commissions)
- **L2** — SSE income covers subsistence in the person's own country
- **L3** — SSE income replaces the prior salary
- **L4** — SSE income substantially exceeds the prior salary; assets accumulating
- **L5** — the level the teaching gestures at: mastery plus wealth

Recorded per person: apprenticeship start, prior job and income where stated, date of leaving employment if any, ventures attempted, **ventures abandoned**, income by year where stated, current level, and **whether they returned to employment** — that last one being the outcome most likely to be quietly absent from essays.

## Rules, frozen

1. **Leavers are in the denominator.** This matters more here than anywhere else: the people for whom SSE failed are disproportionately the people who left, so scoring current members would measure the survivors of the very process under test. `mira`'s motorbike business failing and `tove`'s cookie stall are SSE outcomes and get scored as such.
2. **Attribution discipline.** "Financially fine" is not the same as "SSE worked". Three examples that will otherwise corrupt the result:
 - `fenn` describes himself as *"97% successfully **pseudo**-self-employed"* in his own words, because he is contracted by a company that provides all his customers — *"I cannot decide to not work today, and if they were to close up shop, I would also be out of business."* That is honest self-assessment and should be scored as he scored it.
 - `wren`'s income is the army. Institutional employment, not SSE.
 - `yar`'s money is two IT jobs — corporate employment, and the largest income in the group.
 So the apprentices who are most solvent are largely solvent *because of the arrangements the teaching tells them to leave.* That inversion is the core thing to measure.
3. **Count abandoned ventures, not just live ones.** A person can report three business starts across five years and be at L1 throughout. Only counting current ventures reads as progress.
4. **A baseline is mandatory.** In the general population roughly a fifth of new businesses fail in the first year and about half within five. For the teaching to be doing anything, its cohort has to beat that — and if the cohort merely matches it, the honest conclusion is that the apprenticeship is not adding to what people would have achieved anyway. Without a baseline this question is unfalsifiable in the *favourable* direction too, which is exactly the flaw being investigated elsewhere in this repo.
5. **Self-reported figures are marked as such**, and separated from figures with corroboration (a signed contract, a client payment described in detail, a rental agreement, a job change confirmed by later logistics).

## The fee structure, stated plainly

The one unambiguously successful self-employment in this ecosystem is the teaching itself: **£150 per hour-long call**, ten or so apprentices, penalties of £50–£350 per missed essay, masterclasses, a book, a YouTube channel. As `yar` puts it, that rate is itself the demonstration — and more so for someone who came from Bhaarat, where it is a multiple of local professional rates. And the money that funds it comes overwhelmingly from **apprentices' employment income** — the corporate jobs they are being urged to leave. Two of the three departures examined in `why-apprentices-leave.md` were people in arrears on those fees: one sold motorbikes to pay, one worked a call centre for six months to clear call debt.

The fair reading has to be included: teaching is real work, a teacher charging for it is not a scandal, and there is evidence of fees being reduced for individuals — one apprentice was told his fee would halve and then become gift-based. But the structural tension is a fact and belongs in the record: **the prescription reduces the income that pays for the prescription**, and no one in the archive has ever raised that.

- **`rook`**: also recently left corporate, per `yar`.

- **`mira`**: her path with heart was **woodcarving**; the motorbikes were a means of making money, not the vocation. Removed when she could not pay fees. L1 and terminated by the fee structure rather than by the venture failing.
- **`yar`**: two IT jobs, no SSE. L0, and the largest income in the group.
- **`arlo`**: the only apprentice the teacher has ever credited with getting rich — *"first apprentice to go from 0 to hero in terms of money"*, Sept 2022. The money came from **trading the stock market**, and he himself doubted it counted: *"I'm not actually running a business, I'm not offering my services to others… but is it, really? Full arc in `why-apprentices-leave.md`. **The flagship success is not SSE, is doubted by its own subject, and was posthumously reclassified as a spiritual failure.**

- **`orin`**: income from **renting out apartments**. Property, not vocation.
- **`kesh` and `isla`**: both did **waiting jobs**. `kesh` was penalised £150 for missed essays in the same period she was planning to sleep in her car for a winter; `isla` was working for a bar owner she also used as boundary-training material.

Two of the apprentices whose vocations were craft or healing — the clearest "path with heart" cases in the archive — are the two who were removed over money.

**The provisional shape of the answer, stated carefully.** Real path-with-heart earnings do exist and must not be written out of the record: `holt`'s €7,000 carving commission and his ~€3,250 share of a product sale are self-employment income from the craft itself, `vela` is paid for dance performances and massage, `wren` has earned from street singing, `tove` sold 80% of a cookie batch. These are genuine, and some are growing.

What is missing is different and more specific: **nobody has yet replaced a living with them.** The incomes that reliably pay the bills across thirty-one people and six years are freelance SEO, a two-day-a-week engineering job, two IT jobs, a rental portfolio, army pay, waiting tables, a call centre, a contracted tour-guiding job, and stock trading — employment, contract work, or capital in every case. The path-with-heart earnings sit on top of those as episodic lump sums rather than under them as a floor. And the one case the teacher ever named as a money success was trading, by a man who doubted it counted and was later called a struggling victim.

So the question for Monday is not "has anyone earned anything from their path with heart" — several have. It is **"has anyone reached L3, where the vocation replaced the job, and stayed there for a year"** — and that is answerable in a unit nobody can reinterpret.

**What the sketch suggests, and what would refute it.** The pattern to test is that SSE in this group produces process milestones and occasional lump sums rather than replaced incomes, that the solvent members are solvent through employment or institutions, and that nobody has reached L4. It would be refuted by two or three apprentices at L3+ on SSE income alone, sustained over more than a year — and `sable` and `rook` leaving corporate this year is precisely the natural experiment that will show it either way.

## RESULTS — run 2026-08-17

Run with `scripts/sweep_sse.py` over the complete corpus (2,002 essays + 34,609 messages).
**246 monetary figures exist in the entire archive and all 246 were read.**

### First, what the method cannot see — stated before the results, per `commitments-recall-check.md`

**Income that was never posted is invisible.** Fees, arrears and earnings are largely
discussed on the paid calls, and no call is in the archive. So **every ladder score below is
a floor, not a measurement**, and "no reported income" never means "no income". This limit is
larger here than in the other studies, because money is private in a way that essays are not.

### The promise: there is no stated timescale, anywhere

The design file said the study is worthless without the claim, and predicted that if no
timescale was ever stated *that is itself the finding*. It was not stated.

**In six years the teacher mentions SSE, "path with heart" or "slave job" in twelve messages
total.** Not one attaches a date, a horizon, or a success criterion. There is no "within N
years", no income target, no definition of what would count as having arrived. `yar`'s
recollection of "vague, five to ten years" is not contradicted by the archive, and it is not
supported by it either — the archive contains no horizon at all.

Two of those twelve messages are the same link, posted in 2024 and again in 2025: *"Follow
this link for self education on SSE"* — pointing at **an Instagram daily-copywriting
account.** That is the sum of the instructional material for SSE in the chat archive. It is
worth noting alongside `holt`'s own assessment in Dec 2025 that *"Copywriting, especially
entry level, is disappearing very quickly."*

**Consequence, stated plainly:** a prescription with no timescale and no success criterion
cannot fail. That is not an incidental gap. It is the structural feature that makes this the
one prescribed outcome denominated in money and *still* unfalsifiable on its own terms.

### The teacher's own assessment, six years in

The sharpest datum in this study is his, not mine. 2026-04-27, unprompted, to the group:

> *"Tbh- in every call it's extremely rare for any apprentice to be interested or involved in
> their explorations & insights in presence. **Pretty much all the time, the talk is about how
> to earn more pocket money by doing something that's mildly more interesting than a slave
> job.**"*

That is the person who set the goal describing what the pursuit of it amounts to across the
whole cohort. It agrees with the ladder scoring below, and it was said without any prompting
from an outcome study.

### The flagship success was withdrawn by the teacher himself

`arlo` was credited in Sept 2022 as *"first apprentice to go from 0 to hero in terms of
money"*. On **2022-10-12** `arlo` reports: *"Based on my last call with Nagual Anaam, I know
now that **the money I made in the stock market was not from SSE**."* So the one money success
the teacher ever named was reclassified as not-SSE by the teacher, six weeks later, before
`arlo` left in December. The archive's only "0 to hero" case is therefore not an SSE outcome
by the ruling of the person who made the claim.

### The answer to the question this file was opened to ask

> *"Has anyone reached L3, where the vocation replaced the job, and stayed there for a year?"*

**No — and the finding is more specific than a no.** Five people in the archive have ever held
self-employment at subsistence level or better: `thea`, `dov`, `bram`, `gale` and `fenn`.

**Every one of the first four was already self-employed before joining the apprenticeship.**
`thea`'s coaching business was four years old at intake; `dov` and `bram` arrived as business
owners; `gale` arrived as a working actor. The fifth, `fenn`, is contracted by a company that
supplies all his customers and scores himself 97% *pseudo*-self-employed.

**Not one person in six years moved from employment into salary-replacing self-employment
while in the apprenticeship.** The ones at L2–L3 arrived there. And **four of those five left**
— Dec 2022, Mar 2023, Sept 2023, Dec 2023.

Meanwhile the incomes that actually pay the bills across the cohort are: two IT jobs, a
two-day-a-week engineering job, a rental portfolio, army pay, farm labour, waitressing, a
call centre, contracted tour guiding, babysitting, freelance copywriting, and corporate
finance. **Employment, institutions, or capital in every case** — the arrangements the
teaching says to leave.

### Ventures attempted and abandoned — rule 3

Counting only live ventures reads as progress, so: `mira`'s Kukuwa-instructor plan (dropped,
CPR certificate already bought), `mira`'s Breathwork replacement (never mentioned again),
`isla`'s yoga business (still "my dream" after 3½ years), `tove`'s traditional-healing path
(no income in a year of pursuit), `wynn`'s pre-apprenticeship SSE (failed, four years of
unpaid taxes), `wren`'s street singing (superseded by the army), `sable`'s art (net-negative
so far), `kesh`'s BJJ teaching (barter). **Eight abandoned or stalled ventures against zero
salary replacements.**

### The baseline — rule 4, and it cuts the other way

In the general population roughly a fifth of new businesses fail inside a year and about half
within five. **The cohort cannot be compared to that baseline, because almost nobody started
a business.** What the archive shows is not a cohort of founders failing at ordinary rates; it
is a cohort that largely **did not launch** — market stalls, occasional commissions, barter,
and plans held for years without a first customer. On the specific question rule 4 was written
to answer, the honest conclusion is that the apprenticeship is **not adding to what these
people would have achieved anyway**, and for the two people removed over money it subtracted:
`mira` was selling motorbikes to pay fees when she was expelled for not affording them.

### What would refute this

Two or three apprentices reaching L3 on SSE income alone and holding it past a year. `sable`
and `rook` both left corporate employment in 2026 and are the live test; their income
trajectories through 2027 are the most informative data this question will ever get. Revisit
in 2027 — and note in advance that the current picture predicts they return to employment or
to contract work, because that is what every previous case did.
