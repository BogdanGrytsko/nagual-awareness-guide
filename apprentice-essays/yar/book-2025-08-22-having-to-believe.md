# Having to believe

*Weekly essay shared in the apprentice group on 2025-08-22. Reflections on Tales of Power, chapter 4, written during a solo trip to Bhaarat. This is the first of the two essays the 5–10% gift case starts from — see `analysis/frame-vs-evidence-log.md`.*

> "The basic difference between an ordinary man and a warrior is that a warrior takes everything as a challenge, while an ordinary man takes everything either as a blessing or as a curse."

I can see now that when it isn't about work I become a last minute hustler. I've postponed printing my Indian E-visa till the last day before flying, and on the last day it turned out that it's a public holiday in Spain and most places don't work. I've started rushing to get to a copy center before it closes, drove my E-kickscooter the wrong lane, got stopped by the police and later on I've fallen down while I was driving it with 1 hand and checking the maps with another. Only after that I slowed down, copy centers closed anyway. Life told me to slow down, I didn't listen so life repeated again. The lesson is obvious, slow down and don't postpone things till the last day. It all turned out fine, my wife recommended a china shop which always works and has a printer.

I made it! I've got to Delhi! It's the first time in my life when I'm traveling completely alone and no one is waiting on the other side. In Delhi I'm living near the main Bazar road. When I first went out (On Sunday) for a walk I experienced some fear and shock. So many people, so many try to reach and sell something to me. I ignored them, and just kept walking at my own pace. I felt very alert, perhaps the most alert in my entire life, not relaxed though. I kept attention on my pockets, where my phone and room key were, on the surroundings and partially checking feeling-from-the-back - e.g. if someone is following me. Turns out no one does it here, the tactic is to walk along your side and start a small talk - about the direction or how crazy Delhi is or where the spice market is etc.

On Monday I was driving with a private chauffeur, a completely different experience. After I exchanged dollars and got some physical local money in my pocket I felt sooo much safer. My belief in the power of money is super strong and makes me feel safe. I've seen a black cube in Gandhi's burial place and it surprised me (black cubes got here too? wtf). India Gate felt like a powerful place\monument. In the end, to my surprise, I was taken to the shop. I liked and bought a short kurta (a nice local shirt), and then as I was about to leave the salesman passed me to another one, selling suits. I politely listened for a bit, no, I don't want a suit, I went away. Yet another seller, showing me the jewelry. At that point I felt like I'm a whore passed from one man to another, wtf!! I just left, no more politeness from me. I understand that I'm just wasting my time by being polite and I'm taken advantage of.

On Tuesday I went by foot again, visited Old Delhi. I felt like difficulty has ramped up, even more people, and this time a beggar kid is following me, and just ignoring doesn't help. And now the second beggar is joining! I said to them out loud "Get lost!!!" 2 times, and they vanished. While I was peacefully sitting in the park some guy came to me, shouting and pointing phone at me. I left the location and told him very loudly "Don't talk to me!!!". I see it as a training in not being nice and polite.

On the way to Agra I noticed many many billboards, all of them displaying "white indians". Indians that look like white people, the magic of photoshop. If I recall correctly Nagual told about this in one of the MCs - Indians are brainwashed to want to be like white, white is good. The guide also told me that although Indians have Ayurveda, they prefer to go to the doctor and get a pill. I noticed many many gurus on billboards (with red dots and everything), competition is fierce. As we stopped near one temple, my driver said that phones are not allowed inside. I turned it into airplane mode, but that wasn't allowed. I felt fear, I didn't want to leave my phone with my driver whom I knew for 3 hours, I didn't feel safe at all. This tells me that I'm exactly like an average man, I don't feel safe without my magic device. Anyhow, I said NO and we left. Tadj Makhal itself is very beautiful, I felt like WOW, this is amazing.

In Jaipur I visited Amber Fort, a very beautiful and serene place, surrounded by mountains and forests, with a big lake to the side. My guide told me that the maharaja had a separate place to grow saffron - "ancient viagra". Now I'm eager to try it out. I also fed and washed and hugged an elephant for the first time - it felt great, those creatures are huuge. One last interesting fact that the guide told me - "GOD" is an acronym for Generator Operator Destroyer - three main hindu gods. I was surprised and I now think that "GOD" is just repackaging and rebranding of the same old thing.

I now understand that I've taken many things for granted in my life. Like traffic lights - they make life so much easier when I'm crossing the road as a pedestrian! In India you just cross, do your best, good luck. And the silence of the streets. I appreciate silence so much, it's a blessing when no one is horning. And tap water - In Spain I can just boil and drink it, amazing! In India it's polluted with heavy metals and pesticides. On the third day I noticed etiquettes on the bottles - they are usually either Cola or PepsiCo. I recalled an old YT video about how corporations operate(d) in Africa - first they poison the ground and the water with all-the-stuff, and then they make money from selling water to local people. Now I have seen it with my own eyes.

I feel relaxed and alert right now.
I will die and I don't know when.
