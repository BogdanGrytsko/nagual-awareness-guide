# Be Right & Ignore Consequences

*Shared in the apprentice group on 2025-09-13, on a TNZ video Nagual Anaam assigned me directly after the group conversation about the gift — "Bogdan your training here is to avoid taking these questions personally… Watch my latest video on TNZ YT and give an essay on that topic relating to this conversation." This is the essay in which I reclassify my own refusal as self-importance. See `analysis/frame-vs-evidence-log.md`.*

Nagual Anaam tells about a very dangerous trick that self-importance plays on us.

> "Self importance will get you to prefer repeating that over anything else. SI will get you to favor getting your way over considering consequences. Favoring getting your way by insisting on being right. By hook or by crook getting your way."

I see that I've fallen for this trick during the hunt in TAC. I haven't considered the consequences of what I was saying and how I was replying. I have now sat and thought through the consequences. The ultimate consequence would be me becoming an extremely toxic, self-righteous person, without friends and without any will to change anything in my life. As good as dead.

> "What you have to do is to worry about consequences first, and 'being right' - just ignore that. You want to be deadly concerned about consequences at all times."

> "If I insist on being right, what are the consequences? For me and for the other person. I'm gonna drive this person away. I'm gonna deteriorate the situation further, and it's gonna be my fault in the end."

I don't want to be like that. I don't want to deteriorate the situation and to drive the other person away.

Nagual gives an example of driving - how long can one last if he favours being right over consequences? Not long obviously, I imagine that if I were to always drive on the yellow light (but-I'm-right), a mirror person would appear in my life very quickly and we would crash on the crossroads. When I decided a few times in the past to break the rules (like drive on the red light for adrenaline and to not-follow-the-rules) I considered consequences first, what the fine will be etc. Still, there are more, which I didn't know - like after police flags a car they can stop it without a reason. The lesson in this for me is that I'm not as smart as I think I am, I actually often do shallow research and pretend to be better than I really am. Actually often I'm quite stupid, and I'm grateful for Nagual's and TAC's help.

> "Their will has been sapped from them. The will to keep aside their need to be right and to have their way all the time."

I feel relaxed and alert right now.
I will die and I don't know when.
