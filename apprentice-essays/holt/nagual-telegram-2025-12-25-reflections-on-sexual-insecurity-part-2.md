## Reflections

This conversation made things a lot clearer for myself why I act the way I do, so I felt it was useful to write a reflection on it.

What really stayed in my mind is how I hide things away and want to be seen better than I am. It really surprises me because it doesn't make any sense, yet I behave like this. It feels so uncomfortable to honestly admit where I am at; I want to be perceived by Nagual Anaam and the fellow apprentices as a big shot. But Nagual Anaam is the one person I know who is not impressed, but who can actually help me to grow and better myself in any area of my life if I wish to change and grow.

The second thing that stayed in my mind is how I don't take and do not want to take response-ability for the consequences of my actions. It feels that these two are connected: I don't want to take response-ability in my life and so I continue to feel like a victim of the circumstances. Then to not face my cowardice and victimhood, I want to feel good about myself by pretending to be better than I am.

In the TAC Nagual Anaam asked Vela: "By when will you have relinquished your JW GVS? If you could give year & month?"

This made me reflect: For all these years I've been holding onto my GVS that as a child I felt that my mother gave more love to my brother than to me, and I always felt this GVS in myself and how my inner child was feeling like a victim. It always felt that this is 100% true how it happened, and I have all the right in the world to feel this way. But again: I'm not taking response-ability for myself and my actions now: What happened in the past happened in the past, and I can't even trust my memory, but now I'm an adult and I can take care of my inner child and can take care of myself if I choose to, so there is no need for the GVS if I would really want to let it go.

A few days ago I was doing mirror work and at some point let my eyes relax and not focus, and my face shifted. In the past this always gave my body a slight feeling of shock and I got scared, so I always looked away quickly when that happened, but this time I let it be and continued to look at the different face in the mirror. Then I thought, if all these incarnations are happening, at what point will I take response-ability for myself and that I'm here? At some point, I thought, I have to do it anyways, sooner or later. And what kind of life is this, to live and not take response-ability, to be like an offended child who stumps his feet on the floor because he didn't get the toy he wanted. Yes, I haven't decided to be here, or at least I don't remember anything about that, and I have no idea where I came from, who I am, where I am, and I'm in a predator-prey world. Yes I did not decide to come here, but now I'm here, so why would I continue to have this subconscious resistance to life? Why not say: I am here now, so I might as well take response-ability for myself and my life from now on. What kind of life is it to not take the response-ability for the consequences of my actions? There is no dignity in that. No dignity.

I'm an adult now, and I have the power to take charge how I feel in my inner world. For example: Last Sunday Nagual Anaam tasked me to do 100,000 repetitions of "I approve of myself". It feels so good! In the past I've repeated affirmations as fast as possible in my mind, to get to the number I wanted to achieve as fast as possible to then feel like a big shot about myself. This time, I am much slower; after each repetition of "I approve of myself" I wait a few moments and feel how that sinks in, how that changes how I feel, how it changes the energy in my body. Now, it actually makes me feel good saying these affirmations. Whenever I repeat the affirmation, I feel a wave of relief coming over me, and something in me says: Ahh finally, I approve of myself! Finally I approve of myself! Finally, finally, I approve of myself!

Yesterday I went out with a few friends, and we went to a Christmas party of some other friends. The last time I met this other group of friends, I felt a bit insecure because I don't know them very well, and I was afraid of "what will they think of me". It wasn't very strong, but still it was there and strong enough that I didn't fully relax. This time, I decided to internally say "I approve of myself" a few times in this slow way when we arrived and I felt the same insecurity come up, and it completely changed the entire experience this evening for me. This fear of what will they think of me went down all the way, and I felt genuinely comfortable in myself, and had a great time there with lots of laughter as well. This was a wonderful example that yes, I do have the choice to take charge over how I feel. I don't have to feel like an insecure little boy, but instead, I can feel secure in myself.

The eczema still didn't go away this week, so it seems that there is more behind all of this that needs to be released.

After E. had slept with another guy five years ago, Nagual Anaam told me to be very honest with myself and not shut any emotions away, because otherwise I'll have to recapitulate all that later. Turns out I did exactly what Nagual Anaam told me not to do, and the past days this grief and hurt that I've been holding onto started coming out, and my body felt like a lot of crying, so I allowed myself to just release what needs to come up. There is so much stuck energy there that I never looked at, so no wonder the eczema is expressing itself like this, now that S. is visiting soon.

Letting the emotions from the memory from E. come up, and reflecting on why my inner child felt so hurt, I realized some patterns inside me how I wish to receive affection and how I give affection. I realized that until now the way I give affection is by copying how my father gave affection to my sister as a child, and because I have no other reference point outside of that, this is what I copied. For example how I give a hug or stroke the hair with my hand are two that stand out. Now I'm curious to discover my own way how to give affection.

Besides that, the way how I wish to receive affection is that I'm looking for the kind of unconditional type of love from my mother and I unconsciously look to receive that in my sexual relationships. I'll use the term unconditional love for now, although Nagual Anaam has made it clear in the past that it's all mating instinct and there is not unconditional love. A few days ago I contemplated on the desire for this unconditional motherly type love, and I realized how many grudges I held against my mother, and how I felt I didn't receive enough love. But then I realized how much she loves me, and how unconditionally she loves me: During these last years, when I stopped my physics study, went to Sweden, etc., she could absolutely not understand my decisions and thought I'm in a cult around Nagual Anaam, but still she gave so much efforts to understand me, reading self help books like Eckhart Tolle and Louise Hay to try and understand my thoughts and thinking, and she tried in the best way possible that she could. From her perspective, it must have been very hard to see me take a route in life that is completely opposite to everything she perceived as normal, but still, she gave her best to understand me. If that's not proof enough for her motherly love… how could I have asked for more? Yes, she didn't believe in me, but I feel as mother that's not her role, that would have been the role of my father.

This realisation relaxed something in me, and I felt that maybe I don't need to hold this GVS of not feeling loved as a child any longer, because I can change how I see my mother and that she loved me. With that I'm curious to see if that changes anything in the relationship I have with other women.
